\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent debate lets several large language model agents criticise each other's answers, and its knowledge-enhanced variant MADKE adds a shared pool of retrieved passages from which each agent selects evidence. The public implementation of MADKE departs from the published method: the summariser prompt has the question and the debate swapped, a selection written as one list delivers no evidence, failed model calls are scored as answers, and no record links an answer to its evidence. This study proposes MADKE-X, which keeps the debate unchanged and adds four components: an interface layer that reports failed calls and parses replies in any common format, an evidence governor that orders the specified pool by reciprocal rank fusion of the retrieval rank and a question-only BM25 rank, a decision trace with passage citations and a lexical support check, and a verification step limited to one extra call. Each component is defined by equations and measured on five unaltered benchmark files. The governor raises the share of questions with an answer-bearing passage among the first three from \VCovThreeOrigTQA\% to \VCovThreeRrfTQA\% on TriviaQA, \VCovThreeOrigNQ\% to \VCovThreeRrfNQ\% on NQ, \VCovThreeOrigHQA\% to \VCovThreeRrfHQA\% on HotpotQA and \VCovThreeOrigWMQ\% to \VCovThreeRrfWMQ\% on 2WikiMultihopQA (paired exact McNemar test, $p\le\VPvalMax$) and shortens the evidence listing by \VTokRedMean\%. Under identical scripted agents MADKE-X passes all \VInvChecksAll{} pipeline checks, while the release ends only \VStopPctRel\% of its debates in the round of agreement. A debate system can be made traceable and cheaper to query without changing the debate; accuracy with live models is compared under a paired protocol.
\end{abstract}
